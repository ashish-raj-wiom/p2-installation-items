Wiom · Change Spec · v1.0 · PM deliverable · GAP-08

Customer location + voice directions on the CSP / Technician app

The Customer App now captures the install location as a set of structured fields (house number, street, locality, landmark, pincode, city) and also lets the customer record a voice note explaining how to reach the door — "turn left after the white temple, look for the chai shop." None of this reaches the partner today. The technician arrives with just a free-text address line, which in many zones is not enough to find the door. This spec wires the data from where the customer gives it to where the technician needs it.

Author: Ashish Raj  ·  Reviewer: Customer Backend + CSP Backend  ·  Tier: Light
Scope: backend-only. This spec describes the data flow between Customer Backend and CSP Backend so that the CSP / Technician App can display the customer's structured address and voice note. It does not cover the app UI — layout, audio player, icons, fallback states are a separate Surface Contract.
Open items: TBC — exact field name for the voice audio link and how its access works (always-valid vs short-lived) are pending Customer Backend team confirmation.

Quick Check

7-line triage — read this before the rest.
What is changing?
Two pieces of customer-provided information — the structured address (the 8 fields the Customer App captures) and the voice direction (an audio file the customer can record at booking) — are made available to the CSP / Technician app. The data already exists on the Customer Backend side; the change is to forward it to the CSP backend and surface it in the existing partner-facing booking view.
What triggered it?
The Customer App rolled out structured-address capture and voice recording at booking earlier this year. Field technicians have repeatedly reported that the free-text address is not enough in tier-2/3 cities and rural zones, where landmarks are spoken rather than written. Meanwhile the customer's voice note — recorded specifically to help the technician — sits unheard.
Who is affected?
Technicians and partner field staff — they get more usable information at install time. Customers — the effort they put into recording the voice note is no longer silently lost. Ops — fewer "wrong address" install failures and re-visits.
What teams need to act?
Customer Backend team — confirms the field name of the voice audio link and how its access works, and forwards both pieces of information to the CSP backend on booking creation. CSP Backend team — stores both pieces against the install booking and exposes them in the partner-facing API the CSP app already calls. CSP App team — consumes the two new pieces of data (UI rendering is a separate spec).
Out of scope
CSP / Technician App UI (separate Surface Contract). Voice-to-text transcription. Analytics on whether the audio was played. The customer-side capture flow itself (already live). Multi-language audio handling beyond playing the file.
Risk to flag
The voice recording is the customer's voice — there is a privacy dimension. Access to the audio must be restricted to the partner actually assigned to the booking. The customer's consent for the partner to hear it must be confirmed by Legal / Privacy.
What success looks like
When a technician opens a booking that has structured address + voice recorded, they see the address laid out properly and can tap to play the customer's voice note. When either piece is missing — older booking, customer skipped voice — the app degrades cleanly with no error. Customer Backend's existing event stream to CSP backend gains two extra pieces of information; nothing else changes.

A2. Why This Change WHAT

The problem in one paragraph

The Customer App collects useful information at booking — a structured address (house, street, locality, landmark, pincode, city) and an optional voice note where the customer can explain how to find the door. The free-text address that finally reaches the partner is often inadequate, especially in addressing-difficult zones — rural lanes without street names, multi-tenant buildings without floor markers, urban interior pockets where the landmark matters more than the postal address. The structured fields and the voice recording would help the technician, but neither makes it to them today. The result: more wrong-address arrivals, more customer call-outs, more re-visits.

Who experiences it

  • Technicians — first-visit success rate is lower than it could be in addressing-difficult zones. The information the customer provided was discarded by the pipeline before it reached them.
  • Customers — they recorded a voice note assuming someone would hear it. They paid the time cost; the partner never received the file.
  • Ops — "wrong address" rejections show up in install-failure analytics. Each one consumes a slot and a partner trip.

Cost of leaving it as-is

Every booking in an addressing-difficult zone has a structurally higher install-failure rate than it would otherwise. The voice-recording feature on the Customer App becomes a shipped-but-inert surface — present in the product, never closing the loop. As the Customer App's structured capture rolls out to more zones, the gap widens.

OS prescription

None — this is operational data enrichment, not a state-machine change. No OS clause is being violated or restored. The data conceptually belongs to the customer's booking; carrying it forward to the partner is a UX completeness move.

A5. The Delta WHAT

What is expected: One row per piece of change — described in business terms, not API contracts.
What changesTodayAfter this changeWhy
Customer Backend forwards the structured address to CSP backend The Customer App captures eight address fields (house, street number, locality, street, landmark type, address, pincode, city) and saves them on the booking record. The notification Customer Backend already sends to the CSP side at booking creation carries only the free-text address — the structured fields are left behind. The same notification now includes the full structured address object alongside the free-text address. No transformation, no renaming — the partner side sees what the customer typed. The structured fields are how the customer expressed where they live. Sending only the free-text line throws away useful detail. Adding the structured object to the existing notification is the smallest change that closes the loop.
Customer Backend forwards the voice-direction link to CSP backend TBC The Customer App uploads the voice recording to a file-storage location and the link is saved on the booking record. The link never reaches the CSP side. The notification Customer Backend sends to the CSP side includes the link to the voice recording, when one exists. If the customer skipped the recording, the link is simply absent. Same closing-the-loop principle as the address row. The audio is the customer's effort to help the technician; it needs to reach the technician.
CSP Backend stores both pieces against the booking The CSP backend's install booking record carries scheduling, customer name, free-text address, partner-side state. Nothing structured, no audio reference. The CSP backend now also stores the structured address object and the voice link against the booking. Both are optional — older bookings simply won't have them and that's fine. The information needs a home on the partner side so that whenever the partner-facing booking view is fetched, it can carry these along.
The partner-facing booking view returns both pieces The CSP / Technician App calls the partner-side booking-detail endpoint to render the booking screen. It receives scheduling, customer details, address text. Nothing else. The same endpoint now also returns the structured address object and the voice link. The CSP App reads these and decides how to present them (UI is a separate spec). The CSP App already polls or fetches this view today; extending what it returns is the smallest possible surface change for the app. No new endpoint, no new auth model.
Access control on the voice file Not applicable — the audio doesn't reach the partner side today. Only the partner actually assigned to the booking should be able to retrieve the audio. The mechanism depends on how Customer Backend exposes the link (always-valid vs short-lived) — Customer Backend team to confirm. Whichever path is chosen, unassigned parties must not be able to retrieve the audio. The recording is the customer's voice — privacy-sensitive. The control should be tight: the partner who has the booking can hear it, no one else.
For the Customer Backend team to confirm:
  1. The exact name of the voice-audio link field on the booking record (we have assumed a placeholder name for this spec).
  2. How the link works once shared — does it stay valid for the full install window (3 days), is it short-lived and needs refreshing, or does it go through Customer Backend with its own access check? The choice determines whether CSP Backend has to ask Customer Backend for a fresh link each time the partner opens the booking, or can simply pass the link through.
  3. Whether all existing consumers of the booking-creation notification will keep working when these two new pieces of information are added.

B5. Acceptance Criteria — Verification Layer HOW

What is expected: Given / When / Then blocks covering business logic and edge cases. Business logic

LOGIC-1: Happy path — booking has both structured address and voice note
  Real-world: a customer in Gurugram books a connection on the Customer App.
  They fill in all the address fields (house, street, locality, landmark, pincode, city)
  and record a 15-second voice note explaining that the gate is opposite a chai shop.

  Given the customer captured the full structured address on the Customer App
  And   the customer recorded a voice direction that uploaded successfully
  When  Customer Backend fires the booking-creation notification to the CSP side
  Then  the notification payload carries the structured address object verbatim
        (all eight fields the customer entered, untouched)
  And   the notification payload carries the voice-direction link
  And   CSP Backend stores both pieces against the install booking

  When  the partner-facing app fetches the booking detail
  Then  the response includes the structured address object
  And   the response includes the voice-direction link
  And   the existing free-text address field is also still present (no removal)

  Verify:
    • Customer Backend → CSP Backend notification — both new pieces are present in the payload
    • CSP Backend stored state — booking record carries the structured address object and the voice link
    • Partner-facing booking-detail response — returns both new pieces alongside everything it returns today
    • Partner / Technician App — renders the structured address and offers an audio player (UI behaviour is a separate Surface Contract; this spec only verifies the data is reaching the app)
    • Negative — no other partner-side data is removed, renamed, or reordered as a side-effect of adding the new pieces
LOGIC-2: Older booking — predates the Customer App's structured capture rollout
  Real-world: a booking was created before the Customer App rolled out structured
  capture in the customer's zone. The booking has only the legacy free-text address.

  Given the booking pre-dates the Customer App structured-capture rollout
  And   only the legacy free-text address is on the booking record
  When  Customer Backend fires the booking-creation notification
  Then  the notification payload carries the legacy free-text address
  And   the structured address object is absent (or empty)
  And   the voice-direction link is absent

  When  the partner-facing app fetches the booking detail
  Then  the partner sees the free-text address exactly as they do today
  And   no structured-address surface is rendered (nothing to render)
  And   no audio player is offered (nothing to play)
  And   the booking flows through install end-to-end with no pipeline error

  Verify:
    • Notification payload — additive new fields are absent, not present-and-empty in a way that breaks consumers
    • CSP Backend — null / absent values stored; no validation failure on the new pieces
    • Partner-facing response — legacy address field intact; new fields simply not present
    • Install flow end-to-end — the booking can still be slot-proposed, customer-confirmed, technician-assigned, installed
    • Negative — no error path triggered by the absence of the new fields anywhere downstream

Use & edge cases

EDGE-1: Voice link gone stale — short-lived link expired before partner opened the booking
  Real-world: applies only if Customer Backend uses short-lived voice links.
  The partner opens a booking 60 hours after creation; the original link has expired.

  Given Customer Backend issues voice links with a TTL shorter than the install window
  And   the booking was created > TTL ago
  And   the originally-stored voice link would now 404 if accessed
  When  the partner opens the booking and the partner-facing detail is fetched
  Then  CSP Backend detects the stored link is near or past expiry
  And   CSP Backend asks Customer Backend for a refreshed link
  And   a working, freshly-signed link is returned to the partner-facing response

  When  Customer Backend's refresh path is itself unavailable
  Then  CSP Backend returns the booking detail WITHOUT the voice link
        (rest of the booking is returned normally)
  And   no error pop-up is shown to the partner
  And   the booking continues to be actionable for everything that isn't the audio

  Verify:
    • Refresh succeeds — partner-facing response carries a fresh link; partner can play the audio
    • Refresh fails — partner-facing response carries the booking but no voice link;
        partner-app renders the booking normally and simply doesn't offer the audio player
    • No regression on other fields — even when the voice path fails, structured address and everything else still return
    • Negative — partner never sees a broken-file error or a stale link that 404s on play
EDGE-2: Audio file removed from storage — link exists but the file is gone
  Real-world: retention policy or a manual cleanup removed the audio file.
  The link itself exists on the booking record but the underlying file is gone.

  Given the voice link is set on the booking record (or returned freshly)
  And   the underlying audio file has been removed from storage
  When  the partner-facing booking detail is fetched
  Then  the partner-facing response still returns the link as it is
        (CSP Backend does not verify file presence on every fetch)

  When  the partner taps play
  Then  the player handles the file-not-found gracefully
        (same fallback as any audio-playback failure on the app)
  And   no upstream error propagates back to CSP Backend
  And   the rest of the booking remains usable

  Verify:
    • CSP Backend — does NOT check storage presence on every booking-detail fetch
        (cost / latency reason — file-not-found is a partner-app concern, not a backend concern)
    • Partner-facing response — carries the link unchanged
    • Partner / Technician App — renders the booking normally; player shows a generic
        "audio unavailable" treatment on file-not-found (UI behaviour is a separate Surface Contract)
    • Negative — partner doesn't see a hard error or a crash; booking remains actionable
    • Boundary clarification — if retention removes the file, the right correction is
        for Customer Backend to clear the link on its side (future enhancement, out of scope here);
        CSP Backend doesn't need to detect this
